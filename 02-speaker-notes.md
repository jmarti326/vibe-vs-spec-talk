> "And honestly? The numbers are wild."

### Slide: Big Stats
- 92% of US devs use AI tools daily
- 60%+ of new code is AI-generated
- 74% report productivity improvements

**Say:**
> "This isn't a niche thing anymore. This IS how we code now."

### Demo #1 (pre-recorded, 90 sec)

## ACT 2: The Reckoning (9 min)

### Engagement: "Who's been paged?"

**Say:**
> "Quick question — raise your hand if you've ever been woken up at night because of a production issue that traced back to AI-generated code."
> [Pause]
> "If your hand isn't up... give it six months."

### Slide: The Data (big numbers)

**Say:**
> "Here's what the research says, and these numbers are from 2026 — this is fresh data:"
> - "AI code introduces 1.7x more issues in production than human-written code."
> - "62% of AI-generated code samples contain security or design flaws after going through standard review."
> - "Logic errors are 75% more frequent. Not typos. LOGIC errors. The kind that cause outages."

### Three Failure Patterns

**Pattern 1: The Cache Stampede**
> "AI writes perfect cache logic. One key, one TTL, one lookup. Textbook correct. But when that key expires and 500 requests hit at the same millisecond? Every single one goes to the database. Your DB falls over. Your app goes down. Your users see a 503."
> "The AI never thought about concurrency because you never told it to."

**Pattern 2: Silent Data Corruption**
> "This one's nastier. The code works. Tests pass. Monitoring looks green. But once every 10,000 requests, a race condition corrupts a user's balance. Nobody notices for weeks. By then, your data is garbage and you can't roll back."

**Pattern 3: The Retry Storm**
> "AI generates error handling with retries. Reasonable, right? But it retries immediately, with no backoff, no circuit breaker. One downstream service hiccup, and suddenly your entire fleet is hammering a dead service with exponential traffic. Congrats, you turned a blip into a cascading outage."

### Audience Quiz

**Slide: Two code snippets side by side**

**Say:**
> "Pop quiz. I'm going to show you two implementations of the same feature. One was written by a senior engineer. One was generated by AI. Can you tell which is which?"
> [Show code — make it genuinely hard]
> [Reveal answer]
> "That's the problem. AI-generated code LOOKS right. It reads well. It passes reviews. The bugs aren't in the syntax — they're in the ASSUMPTIONS."

### Root Cause (brief)

**Say:**
> "Three root causes. One: the AI has no architectural context beyond your current conversation. Two: it optimizes for 'looks correct' not 'behaves correctly under stress.' Three: our tests validate the happy path, and AI-generated tests... also validate the happy path."

**Transition:** "So do we just go back to writing everything by hand? No. We give AI what it needs to succeed."

## ACT 3: The Way + The Proof (22 min)

### Slide: "Structure, Not Speed Limits"

**Say:**
> "The answer isn't to slow down. It's to THINK first and move fast second. Spec-driven development means: decide what you're building BEFORE you build it. Write down your decisions. Then let AI execute against CLEAR specifications, not vibes."

### Principles (8 min)

**Say:**
> "This isn't a new idea. We've had specs forever. What IS new is applying spec discipline to AI-assisted development. And there are many ways to do it:"

**Mention the spectrum:**
- Lightest: "Write 3 bullet points before prompting AI"
- Medium: Custom instructions files, ADRs, system prompts with constraints
- Full framework: GSD (Get Shit Done)

**Introduce GSD:**
> "GSD is one implementation I want to show you. It's open source, works with Claude Code, Copilot, Cursor, Windsurf — basically everything. And it solves a specific problem: context rot."

> "You know how AI gets worse the longer your conversation goes? That's because the context window fills up and quality degrades. GSD keeps your main session clean by farming heavy work to fresh sub-agents. Think of it like... a senior architect who delegates to specialist contractors, each starting fresh with exactly the blueprints they need."

**The Loop (fast visual):**
> [Show animation: New Project → Discuss → Plan → Execute → Verify → Ship]
> "Six commands. Each does exactly one thing. The key insight is: your DECISIONS happen in discuss and plan. The AI's WORK happens in execute. And verification happens BEFORE you ship. Every. Single. Time."

### Engagement: "What would YOUR spec say?"

**Say:**
> "Let's try this together. I'm about to add Redis caching to a products API. What should my spec include? Shout them out."
> [Take 4-5 answers: TTL? Invalidation strategy? What happens on cache miss? Stampede protection? Fallback behavior?]
> "See? In 60 seconds we covered things AI wouldn't think to ask. THAT is the value of the spec."

### Demo #2: The Showdown (12 min)

**Narrate over pre-recorded Round 1:**
> "Same task. Add Redis caching to /products. First, the vibe coding approach."
> [Show prompt → generation → tests pass → deploy]
> "Looks great. Now let's hit it with 500 concurrent requests where the cache just expired..."
> [Show k6/artillery output: response times spike, errors appear, DB connections maxed]
> "Dead. Cache stampede took it down in 3 seconds."

**Narrate over pre-recorded Round 2:**
> "Same task. GSD approach."
> [Show /gsd-discuss-phase: specifying stampede protection, TTL strategy, fallback]
> [Show /gsd-plan-phase: AI researches mutex patterns, plans probabilistic early expiration]
> [Show /gsd-execute-phase: code with singleflight/mutex pattern]
> [Show /gsd-verify-work: verification confirms concurrent behavior]
> "The spec said 'handle concurrent cache misses.' The AI knew exactly what to implement."

**Live moment:**
> "Now here's the fun part. I have BOTH versions deployed right now. Let me hit them with the same load test, live."
> [Run k6 against both endpoints]
> [Show split-screen results: vibe version crumbles, spec version holds steady]
> "Same AI. Same model. Same developer. The only difference is: one got a spec, one got a vibe."

### Progressive Table Reveal

> [Build row by row]
> "Time to first code — yeah, vibe coding wins. 2 minutes vs 15. No question."
> "But time to PRODUCTION-READY? That's where spec-driven catches up and overtakes. Because you're not spending 2 hours at 2 AM debugging the stampede."

## Honest Limitations (4 min)

### Slide: "When to Vibe"

**Say:**
> "I want to be honest with you. GSD has costs. The spec process adds about 15 minutes per feature. Subagents use more tokens — that's real money. And there's a learning curve."

> "So when should you STILL just vibe code?"
> - "Prototyping. Exploring an idea. You don't know what you want yet."
> - "Hack days. The whole point is speed over quality."  
> - "Throwaway scripts. If nobody's depending on it, who cares."
> - "Learning. Sometimes the fastest way to understand is to build messy and iterate."

> "But the moment someone else depends on your code — users, teammates, a business — that's when you write the spec."

> "And look, it's not binary. Tomorrow, you could just start with three bullet points before your next AI prompt. That alone will improve your output. You don't need a framework to think first."

## ACT 5: Takeaways (5 min)

### Slide: "Your Level, Your Takeaway"

**Say:**
> "If you're early in your career: vibe coding is an incredible learning tool. But treat it like a tutor, not a replacement for understanding. Ask the AI WHY it made each choice."

> "If you're mid-career: this is your superpower. The developers who can write clear specs and verify AI output are going to be the most valuable engineers in any organization. That's the job now."

> "If you're senior: your judgment is MORE valuable than ever. The AI can implement. YOU decide what to implement, how it should behave under stress, what the failure modes are. You're the architect. AI is the contractor."

### Closing engagement

**Say:**
> "Last question. On a scale of 1 to 5 — 1 being never, 5 being always — how often do you write any kind of spec or plan before prompting AI?"
> [Let them think]
> "Whatever your number is... try bumping it up by 1 this week. That's it. One notch."
